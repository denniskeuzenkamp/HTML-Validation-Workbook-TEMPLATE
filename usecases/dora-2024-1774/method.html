• Basis. The Global Internal Audit Standards (The Institute of Internal Auditors, 2024). Standard 13.4 Evaluation Criteria – the normative documents provide the criteria; 13.6 Engagement Work Program – the Validation sheet is the test programme; 14.1 Gathering Information – the Source register records every document; 14.2 Analyses and Potential Findings – evidence must be sufficient, reliable, relevant and useful; 14.3 Evaluation of Findings – conclusions and auditor overrides; 14.6 Engagement Documentation – workpaper export (CSV/JSON); Standard 4.2 Due Professional Care – the auditor applies professional scepticism to AI output. Verify numbering against the published Standards before use in formal workpapers.
  • Normbuilder Engine. Converts the uploaded normative documents into structured audit requirements (here: one requirement per Article, with source reference and text) and uses an LLM to define four requirement classes for each: Control Design (what an appropriate control, process, policy, procedure or governance arrangement looks like), Monitoring the Effectiveness (how the control is monitored to remain effective), Demonstrate by Evidencing (which evidence is needed for TD, DE and OE) and Ownership (RACI: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed). Until the LLM has run, the sheet shows clearly labelled template drafts.
  • Matching Engine. Splits evidence into chunks (size and overlap in AI Setting), embeds them, embeds each requirement class and ranks chunks by cosine similarity. Evidence is matched to the requirement classes, not only to the raw requirement text. Matches below the similarity threshold are discarded. Output is a list of candidate matches.
  • Embedding limitations. Similarity measures topical closeness, not compliance. A high score can attach to a policy that says the opposite; a low score can miss relevant evidence expressed in different words. Scores are not comparable across models, chunk sizes or languages. Chunks may cut a control description in half. The local fallback is lexical (word overlap), not semantic. Every candidate match needs review of the underlying text.
  • Test of Design (TD): does the control, process, policy or governance arrangement exist and is it documented or identifiable? Design Effectiveness (DE): is the design appropriate, complete and capable of addressing the requirement or risk if operated as intended? Operating Effectiveness (OE): did the control, monitoring activity or ownership responsibility operate as designed during the period under review? The three results are never merged into one.
  • Evidence eligibility. Assessed before any conclusion. Policies, procedures, process descriptions, RACI matrices, monitoring frameworks and risk-control matrices are usually suitable for TD, and for DE if they carry sufficient design detail (scope, owner, frequency, criteria, approval). Logs, tickets, approval records, test results, monitoring reports and other dated records are usually suitable for OE. Design-only evidence is never used to conclude OE. Operating evidence is not used for TD or DE unless it also describes the control design. Missing evidence is not compliance.
  • Auditor review and professional judgement. AI-assisted validation supports the auditor and does not replace auditor judgement. AI assumptions and evidence limitations are listed in the Evidence gaps column. Each row has an auditor review flag, override selectors for TD, DE and OE, and a notes field. Period under review, sampling, population completeness and reliability of the evidence source are matters for the auditor.
  • Scope note. The regulation text loaded in this workbook comes from the supplied PDF of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1774 (Titles I–IV, Articles 1–42). Title II applies to financial entities under DORA; Title III applies to financial entities referred to in Article 16(1) of DORA. Confirm which Title applies to the entity under audit and mark non-applicable requirements as such.
